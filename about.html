Metrics

The journal scores follow the recursive citation-network method of Bergstrom, West and Wiseman (2008). Imagine a reader who keeps following references from one journal to another, and now and then jumps to a random journal. Journals that this reader visits often carry more weight, and a citation from a journal with more weight counts for more than one from a peripheral journal. Journal self-citations are left out.

  • Network Factor (NF): a journal’s share of all citation flow within a journal universe, on a scale that sums to 100. It grows with the number of articles a journal publishes. The Network Factor follows an Eigenfactor®-like methodology.
  • Article Network Score (ANS): the NF divided by the journal’s share of articles, so it measures influence per article. The article-weighted average is 1; an ANS of 2 means twice the average influence per article. The Article Network Score follows an Article Influence® Score-like methodology.

For score year t, the scores use citations made in year t to articles and reviews published in the five years before (t−5 to t−1). Duplicate records of the same journal in OpenAlex are combined before the calculation.

‘Eigenfactor’ and ‘Article Influence’ are registered trademarks of the University of Washington. The abbreviations EF and AIS refer to the official metrics.

Reference
Bergstrom, C. T., West, J. D., & Wiseman, M. A. (2008). The Eigenfactor™ metrics. Journal of Neuroscience, 28(45), 11433–11434. DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0003-08.2008.

Journal universes

Only journals in the chosen universe, and citations between them, take part in the network score calculation. The same journal can therefore have different scores in different universes.

  • Norwegian Register: the journals approved at Level 1 or Level 2 in the Norwegian Register for Scientific Journals, Series and Publishers. The register is reviewed by disciplinary expert panels and maintained every year, which makes it an open, curated journal list.
  • OpenAlex: all sources that OpenAlex classifies as journals and where at least one open score is available. This is a much broader list.

Filtered and Raw scores

Open datasets are not perfect. Sometimes OpenAlex does not record references for publications. That can mean a publication really has no bibliography, but also that its references are missing from the data. Filtered scores only count publications with at least one recorded reference; Raw scores count all of them. The website shows filtered scores by default. 'Pubs. w/out refs.' shows the share of eligible publications of a journal that are recorded without a reference.

The research behind it

This study compares open scores calculated from OpenAlex with a benchmark from a commercial citation index, for score years 2023 and 2024. It separates two choices that open reconstructions make at the same time: which publications count (Raw or Filtered) and which journal universe is used. Its main findings are that filtered scores agree better with the benchmark in every universe and that the Norwegian Register gives results that are closest to the benchmark. This setting can be chosen as preset "Close to EF & AIS".

Score years and their data

What is what?

Each data run recalculates score years with the OpenAlex and Norwegian Register data available at that moment. OpenAlex keeps adding and correcting references, so the same score year can differ slightly between runs.

A live year follows the newest run and can still change. A frozen year always comes from the shown run and no longer changes, so it can be cited safely. Twelve months after the end of the score year its status changes from live to frozen. For example, 2025 is updated live with every run until the end of 2026. At that point its status and metrics are frozen. Every run stays available for download.
